> Accurate gravitational-wave (GW) templates from numerical-relativity (NR)
> simulations are essential for GW science. These templates are typically
> computed at finite radii and extrapolated to future null infinity $$\mathscr{I}^+$$,
> the asymptotic boundary of spacetime. However, this procedure is known to not
> accurately capture momentum fluxes and memory effects, limiting the accuracy
> of waveform models and data analyses that rely on them. Cauchy-characteristic
> evolution (CCE) instead integrates Einstein's equations along outgoing null
> hypersurfaces from a Cauchy simulation's finite-radius worldtube to
> $$\mathscr{I}^+$$. Its accuracy, though, has been limited by initial data that produce
> long-lived spurious transients, which contaminate several cycles of the
> waveform emitted by the binary coalescence.  We identify incorrect initial
> angular coordinates as the source of these transients. We construct initial
> data that matches the Cauchy worldtube data through second order in the radial
> derivatives and solve for the angular coordinates consistent with the
> resulting radial profile. These improvements remove the unphysical transient:
> a 10% modulation of a binary black hole's (2,2) mode amplitude lasting
> over 1000M disappears, and the late-time tail of a head-on black hole
> collision is recovered. The construction is independent of the Cauchy
> formulation and allows archived simulations to be reprocessed with CCE, paving
> the way for improved GW catalogs.
